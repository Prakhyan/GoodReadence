About the book

"The Jerusalem Sinner Saved; or, Good News for the Vilest of Men" by John Bunyan is a religious treatise written in the mid-17th century. The work reflects on the theme of redemption, focusing on the idea that even the most wretched sinners can find grace and forgiveness through Christ, with a particular emphasis on the city of Jerusalem as a symbol of spiritual decay and corruption.

About John Bunyan

1628–1688

John Bunyan was an English writer and nonconformist preacher. He is best remembered as the author of the Christian allegory The Pilgrim's Progress, which also became an influential literary model. In addition to The Pilgrim's Progress, Bunyan wrote nearly sixty titles, many of them expanded sermons.
