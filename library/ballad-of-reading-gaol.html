About the book

"The Ballad of Reading Gaol" by Oscar Wilde is a poem written in 1897, after his release from prison following a conviction for gross indecency.

The poem narrates the execution of Charles Thomas Wooldridge, a fellow prisoner who murdered his wife, weaving together objective storytelling with symbolic identification among all convicts. Wilde explores themes of punishment, brutalization, and shared humanity through the haunting refrain "Yet each man kills the thing he loves." Initially published anonymously under his cell number "C.3.3.," the poem became an unexpected success.

About Oscar Wilde

1854–1900

Oscar Wilde was an Irish writer celebrated for his wit, fairy tales and plays. The Picture of Dorian Gray is his only novel. His other works include The Importance of Being Earnest, The Happy Prince and The Ballad of Reading Gaol, spanning comedy, beauty and painful reflection.
